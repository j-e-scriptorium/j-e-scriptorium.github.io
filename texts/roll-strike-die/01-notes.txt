# [roll-strike-die/notes] Notes
From: Roll, Strike, Die, and Other Poems (2021), by Ludovico Ambrosius
URL: https://j-e-scriptorium.github.io/roll-strike-die.html#notes

¶1 The cover illustration is a composite of Pieter Bruegel the Elder's *The Beekeepers and the
Birdnester*, Piet Mondrian's *Dune III*, and an anonymous drawing from a medieval manuscript.

¶2 This title of *Same Flowers, Same Bees* alludes to a poem by Charles Wright. The illustration is
Odilon Redon’s *Two Young Girls among Flowers*.

¶3 “The Abduction of Kore” is for MCS.

¶4 “Go to the Bee” adapts passages from *Proverbs*, the *Aeneid*, and Pius XII’s Address to
Beekeepers.

¶5 “Bees Bow Down” owes its title to a suggestion from Nicholas.

¶6 “Platonic Drone” is for ELS, and adopts passages and arguments from the *Phaedo*.

¶7 The title of *This Twittering World* alludes to a poem by T. S. Eliot. The illustration is Paul
Klee’s *Twittering Machine*.

¶8 The final stanza of the second part of “November Twenty Sixteen” quotes from the Harvard
Sentences.

¶9 “Damasked Eliezer Addresses the Basilisk and Other Demons” imagines internet rationalist and
LessWrong founder Eliezer Yudkowsky’s argument against the Roko’s Basilisk thought experiment.

¶10 “The Economist Visits Damasked Eliezer’s Workshop” dramatizes the AI-Foom Debate that took place
between Eliezer Yudkowsky and economist Robin Hanson.

¶11 “Breed Havoc (The Dervish Runs Out of Steam)” was loosely inspired by the accelerationist
philosopher Nick Land, and rearranges the words of Matthew Arnold’s “Dover Beach.”

¶12 “Summer Solstice, 2020” dramatizes psychiatrist and blogger Scott Alexander’s farewell message
on his long-time website SlateStarCodex.

¶13 Two of “Three Rood Screens” consist of ekphrases upon videos posted to Twitter.

¶14 The illustration accompanying *Salt Spray Gets in Your Eyes* is Plate #4 from David Jones’s
*Ancient Mariner* engravings.

¶15 “Aspidochelone” benefited from the critical comments of R. Cam.

¶16 Many of the sonnets in this part were spurred on by competition from Alec and Jane, and
benefited from their comments during the writing process.

¶17 “Roll, Strike, Die” is for SMS. It includes a versification of the rules to Str!ke Dice.
